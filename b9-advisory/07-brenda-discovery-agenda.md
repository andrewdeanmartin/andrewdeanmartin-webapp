# 07 — Brenda Discovery Agenda

**For:** 60-90 minute conversation between Andrew (advisor) and Brenda (B9 founder/CEO)
**Goal:** Get the 8 gating answers that determine Phase 2 sequencing of the AI portfolio
**Format:** Pre-read sent ahead; conversation-based, not slide-driven
**Output:** Annotated notes that update [`04-USE-CASE-PORTFOLIO-V2.md`](./04-USE-CASE-PORTFOLIO-V2.md) sequencing

## Why this conversation exists

Without these answers, the Phase 2 portfolio is unprioritized. The 8 questions below each directly map to specific use cases being promoted, demoted, or killed. **This is the work that determines if RFP automation leads, or talent experience leads, or real-time ops leads, or finance leads.**

This is NOT:
- A sales conversation
- A pitch for paid work
- A demo of any tool
- A request for budget approval

This IS:
- An ask for her honest read on the business
- An exchange that benefits her even if she does nothing with the answers
- A working session that makes the package useful

## Meeting structure (90 min, with timing)

### 0:00 — 0:10 — Open
**Goal:** Set context, confirm this is just-to-help

- Quick acknowledgment that this is unpaid / no agenda beyond being useful
- Reconfirm what you've been working on: "I built two prototypes, then talked to my AI tools about what's missing, then ran some research. Here's where I landed."
- Set the meeting boundary: "I have 8 specific questions whose answers will let me actually be useful to you. The rest is mine to figure out."
- Brenda's chance to ask anything before diving in

### 0:10 — 0:25 — Question 1 (Sales Mix / RFP %)
**THE gating question.** Goes first because it shapes the most.

> "What % of B9's pipeline last 12 months came from RFP / procurement / supplier-registration channels (vs warm intros, repeat clients, referrals)?"

Listen for:
- Rough magnitude (5% / 20% / 50% / 80%)
- Whether RFPs are growing as a channel or shrinking
- Whether B9 is on key procurement portals already (WBENCLink, Supplier.io, TealBook, individual Fortune 500 supplier portals like Walmart Supplier Network or Coca-Cola Supplier Portal)
- Anecdote: any specific recent RFP win/loss

**What this answer determines:**
| Brenda's answer | Phase 2 implication |
|---|---|
| **>20% RFP** | Group A (RFP cluster) leads Phase 2 — ship NEW-10, NEW-11, NEW-13 first |
| **10-20% RFP** | Ship NEW-13 (Tier 1 Spend Reporting) immediately; defer NEW-10, NEW-11 to Phase 3 |
| **<10% RFP** | Group A demoted; ship Group B (real-time ops) or Group C (account expansion) first; pitch RFP automation only as opportunity to grow that channel |

Follow-up if useful: "Are there RFPs you wish you could compete for but don't have bandwidth?"

### 0:25 — 0:35 — Question 2 (Strategic Ambition)
**Sets the frame for everything else.**

> "What's your 3-year ambition for B9?"

Listen for:
- Lifestyle (current scale, better margins, less stress)
- Growth (2-3x revenue, more cities, more headcount)
- Platform (B9 becomes the operating system other agencies run on)
- Exit (grooming for acquisition)
- Legacy (mentorship, training next gen)
- Something else entirely

**What this answer determines:**
| Brenda's ambition | Implication for portfolio |
|---|---|
| **Lifestyle / better margins** | Prioritize efficiency + cost reduction (Finance cluster, NEW-17 QBR brief, NEW-24 multimodal recap for premium positioning). Lower-volume use cases preferred. |
| **2-3x growth** | Prioritize throughput multipliers (NEW-10 RFP, NEW-16 expansion signals, NEW-20 real-time no-show recovery enables more events). Higher-build use cases acceptable. |
| **Platform play** | This package isn't the right scope — B9 needs different conversations about market positioning, fundraising, product strategy. Honest pushback: "This portfolio is for running B9 better, not for becoming a platform." |
| **Exit-grooming** | Prioritize signals that show buyer-attractive operating maturity (Tier 1 reporting NEW-13, expansion signals NEW-16, real-time ops NEW-20). Compliance posture (Section 5) becomes pre-due-diligence. |

This is also where you can plant the "want me to think harder about this separately?" seed if she signals confusion about her own direction.

### 0:35 — 0:45 — Questions 3 + 4 (Scale + Pain)
Combined because they relate.

> "Roughly how many retained / repeat clients does B9 work with in a typical quarter? And how many 'active' 1099 talent are in the roster?"

Listen for:
- Client side: <10 / 10-30 / 30-100 / 100+
- Talent side: <100 / 100-500 / 500-2,000 / 2,000+

> "What's your single biggest operational pain right now? Whatever you'd change tomorrow if you could."

Listen for: the actual unprompted answer. Don't suggest. Just listen.

**What these answers determine:**
| Scale signal | Phase 2 implication |
|---|---|
| 30+ retained clients | NEW-16 (Expansion Signals) and NEW-17 (Pre-QBR Brief) become high-value |
| <15 retained clients | NEW-16 less critical; NEW-17 still shipped |
| 500+ active talent | NEW-02 (FAQ Chatbot) ROI compounds; consider Group D early |
| <200 active talent | NEW-02 lower priority — coordinators can handle the volume manually |

| Pain signal | Phase 2 priority |
|---|---|
| Pain in cash flow / billing | ROI-11/12 priority (already Phase 1); validates that cluster |
| Pain in talent retention or supply | NEW-02 (FAQ Chatbot) moves up; Group D priority |
| Pain in event-day execution / no-shows | NEW-20 priority |
| Pain in proposal/quote bandwidth | Group A (RFP) confirmed |
| Pain in client retention / repeat-book | NEW-16, NEW-17, NEW-13 priority |
| Pain in founder bandwidth (Brenda overload) | NEW-17 QBR brief specifically; ROI-01 email triage validated |
| Pain in margin / pricing | Phase 3 watchlist priority (Win/loss analysis, margin intelligence) |

### 0:45 — 0:55 — Question 5 (Geography)
**Compliance-driven.**

> "Where does B9 talent live, geographically? Specifically — what's the rough mix of NYC, Illinois, California talent? And what % of bookings happen in those states?"

Listen for:
- NYC talent / NYC bookings share (NYC LL 144 trigger)
- Illinois talent / Illinois bookings share (IL HB 3773 trigger)
- California talent / California bookings share (CA ADMT 2027 trigger)
- NJ talent (NJ ABC Test trigger Oct 2026)

**What this answer determines:**
| Geography pattern | Phase 1 implication |
|---|---|
| Heavy NYC presence (≥15%) | NYC LL 144 bias audit becomes mandatory before ROI-08 ships; budget $5-20K/yr; ship to non-NYC talent first |
| Heavy IL presence (≥10%) | IL HB 3773 notice disclosure on all job postings reaching IL talent immediately |
| Heavy CA presence (≥15%) | CA ADMT 2027 design starts now; pre-use notice + opt-out + access rights built in |
| National diversification (no state dominant) | Standard design rules apply; consider building compliance posture for all 4 states from day 1 |

### 0:55 — 1:05 — Question 6 (Tech Stack)
**Determines augment vs. buy patterns.**

> "What's your current tech stack? StaffConnect for ops, QuickBooks for finance, what for CRM and comms? Any 6-12 month plans to change anything?"

Listen for:
- StaffConnect (confirmed?) and version / API access level
- Accounting: QuickBooks / Xero / FreshBooks?
- CRM: HubSpot / Salesforce / none?
- Comms: Gmail / Outlook / Slack / Teams?
- Phone / SMS: dedicated number / Twilio / native phones?
- Calendar: Google / Outlook?
- Document storage: Google Drive / Dropbox / SharePoint?
- Any planned migrations or replacements?

**What this answer determines:**
| Stack signal | Implication |
|---|---|
| StaffConnect with API access | Real-time no-show replacement (NEW-20) is feasible at L2 |
| StaffConnect without API access | NEW-20 limited to L1; consider Senegal Software at L3 (replaces StaffConnect or sits alongside) |
| QuickBooks Online + accountant | NEW-13 Tier 1 reporting at L2 via QBO API |
| HubSpot or Salesforce in place | NEW-16 expansion signals + NEW-17 QBR brief at L2-L3 via CRM data |
| No CRM | Add lightweight CRM (Folk, Attio, Pipedrive) as foundational; defer L2 expansion signals |
| Limited budget for new tools | Stay at L0-L1 for everything; ChatGPT Teams as primary AI surface |
| Active appetite for tool consolidation | Frame Phase 2 around fewer-tool augmentation; favor Avoma-tier consolidation over best-of-breed |

### 1:05 — 1:15 — Question 7 (Talent-Side AI Tolerance)
**Gates Group D entirely.**

> "How does your team feel about AI handling talent communications directly? Off-limits, cautious, or open?"

Listen for:
- Brenda's personal stance (some founders are fiercely "no robots talking to my talent")
- Coordinator/ops team stance
- Past experience with talent-facing tech (good or bad)
- Premium positioning concern: "talent expects to talk to a human"
- Specific examples of where AI talent comms feels OK vs not

**What this answer determines:**
| Tolerance signal | Group D implication |
|---|---|
| **Off-limits** | Kill NEW-02; defer all Group D to Phase 3; revisit only after compelling client-side wins |
| **Cautious** | NEW-02 ships in pilot mode (20-30 talent opt-in); design with maximum supportive framing; explicit opt-in mandatory |
| **Open** | NEW-02 ships; Group D moves up; consider NEW-04 (self-service portal) and NEW-06 (engagement health) as Phase 3 follow-ons |

### 1:15 — 1:25 — Question 8 (Success Picture)
**Sets measurement.**

> "If any of this is working 6 months from now, what does success look like? What would have to be true for you to say 'this was worth the time'?"

Listen for:
- Specific metrics she names (hours back, revenue lift, client wins, talent retention)
- Vague aspirations (which need turning into metrics)
- Anti-success signals ("if it makes Brenda invisible to clients, that's a fail")
- Things she explicitly doesn't care about (some founders don't care about cost reduction)

**What this answer determines:**
- Sets the KPI baseline targets for Phase 1 metrics tracking
- Defines the "stop / continue / expand" criteria at month 6
- Flags use cases that don't serve her definition of success — even if they're "good practice"

### 1:25 — 1:30 — Close
**Goal:** Set expectations for what happens next; don't over-commit.

- "Give me a week to revise the portfolio against your answers. I'll send you a focused list — probably 3 things to start with, gated on whatever you tell me here."
- Ask for one thing: "Is there one person on your team — coordinator or ops lead — I should also talk to before finalizing? They'll catch what we miss."
- Reconfirm boundary: "After I send the focused list, you decide what to do with it. If you want to actually pilot anything, that's your call. If you want to think about it, also fine."

## Conversation traps to avoid

| Trap | Avoidance |
|---|---|
| **Selling the portal you built** | Don't mention b9-ai-portal or b9-experience by name. Frame as "concepts I worked through." |
| **Selling AI generally** | If she's skeptical, validate the skepticism. Don't push. The portfolio survives skepticism. |
| **Anchoring on the 14 (or 22)** | Don't lead with use case counts. Lead with her business + her pain. |
| **Vendor name-dropping** | Don't list 12 SaaS tools. She doesn't care. Talk in capabilities, not brands. |
| **Bringing the deck** | No deck. This is a conversation. If you must bring something, bring [`01-EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.md`](./01-EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.md) as a pre-read, not a screen-share. |
| **Compliance lecture** | Don't dwell on regulations. One mention: "There are 3-4 use cases where we need a 30-min call with your employment attorney before we ship." Then move on. |
| **Founder advice** | She's been running this 19+ years. Don't tell her how to think about her business. |
| **Pitching paid work** | This is unpaid. Don't pivot. If she asks "would you do this?" — be honest about your bandwidth and let her decide. |
| **Promising specific ROI** | Use directional language: "industry benchmarks suggest a few hours back per week" — don't claim B9-specific numbers. |
| **Going long** | If she has 60 min, prioritize Q1, Q4, Q7. Skip Q5 (you can email it). |
